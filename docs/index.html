Senel words are built from their meaning: the first consonant is the semantic domain, the vowel the subdomain, the last consonant the item.

Two things English leaves out and Senel requires

How you know it

Every statement ends with one syllable saying where the claim comes from. Translating from English, the translator has to guess — and it tells you when it does.

loI saw it
toI was told
moI infer it
yoestablished knowledge
somy own state or intent

Whether “we” includes you

English we is ambiguous and the ambiguity causes real trouble. Senel has two words and you must pick one.

munwe, including you
monwe, not including you

The same applies to or: bu is “one or both”, pu is “exactly one”.

The cost, shown honestly

Because the item sits in the sound the ear hears least well and the domain sits first, whole families of common words differ only in that first consonant. Hear it wrong and the topic changes. This is the measured price of building words from meaning — not hidden, but part of the lesson.

The full analysis, and why the forms are left this way, is in ROBUSTNESS.md.
